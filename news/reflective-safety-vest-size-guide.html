Reflective Safety Vest Size Guide: Measure Your Chest and Pick the Right Size

2026-11-20

Reflective safety vest size guide: measure your chest over what you will wear, find your size, and check the fit before you order for a crew.

A reflective vest that fits stays closed, lies flat and stays visible; a vest that does not fit gaps at the front, rides up, or hangs past the knees — and a vest that is not worn is not seen. Measure your chest over what you will wear under the vest, find your size in the table below, then check the fit before you order for a crew. The whole job takes two minutes, and it is the most useful two minutes in vest buying.

1. How to measure your chest

Take a soft, flexible tape measure — the cloth kind, not a metal one, because a metal tape does not follow the shape of the body. Wrap it around the widest part of the chest, under the arms and across the shoulder blades, keeping the tape level all the way around. Pull it snug without squeezing, and read the number in centimetres to the nearest centimetre.

Measure over what you will wear under the vest, not over a T-shirt if the job wears a jacket. A vest that fits over a T-shirt in summer is a vest that does not close over a winter jacket — and the measurement taken over the jacket is the one that decides the size. Measure twice. If the two readings differ, take the average, or measure a third time. If the measurement falls between two sizes, take the larger one: a vest a little loose still closes and still shows its reflective bands, while a vest a little tight gaps at the front and the bands pull out of line.

Write the number down and send it with your inquiry, not just a size letter. A size letter means different things on different charts, but a chest measurement in centimetres travels across every size system without confusion. The supplier can then confirm which size matches that measurement for the vest you order — and if your crew's chests span a wide range, the measurements tell the supplier how many of each size to send, instead of a guess.

2. The size table: chest measurement to vest size

Vests are usually sold in combined sizes — S/M, L/XL, 2XL/3XL, 4XL/5XL — rather than single letters, because a vest is worn over other clothing and a combined size covers two single sizes at once. The table below is the published sizing guide for vests from a high-visibility workwear maker, and it is the table to start from:

Read the table by the centimetre column. A 100 cm chest is S/M. A 120 cm chest is L/XL. A 140 cm chest is 2XL/3XL. A 155 cm chest is 4XL/5XL. The inch column is the same ranges converted — 36 inches is 91 cm, 44 inches is 112 cm — so either column gives the same answer, and the centimetre column is the one to send with the inquiry.

The table is a starting point, not a guarantee. Vest cuts differ between makers: one maker's S/M is another maker's M/L, and a vest cut to carry tools and gear runs wider at every letter than a plain mesh vest. The measurement is what travels; the letter is what varies. That is why the article keeps coming back to the same point: measure the chest, then check the maker's chart for the vest you are actually ordering.

3. Single-letter sizes are different — check the maker's chart

Some vests are sold in single letters — S, M, L, XL, 2X, 3X, 4X, 5X — and those charts are narrower at every step. One published spec sheet for a mesh vest gives S 36–38 inches, M 38–40, L 40–44, XL 44–46, 2X 46–50, 3X 50–54, 4X 54–58 and 5X 58–62, with garment lengths from 25 inches at S to 30 inches at 5X. Another published user sheet for a high-visibility vest gives the same sizes in centimetres: S 92–96, M 100–104, L 108–112, XL 116–122, 2XL 124–132, 3XL 136–144 and 4XL 148–152.

The three charts agree where it matters. All three put a small chest at about 91–92 cm — the combined table starts S/M at 91 cm, the single-letter spec sheet starts S at 36 inches (91 cm), and the centimetre sheet starts S at 92 cm. The disagreement is only about where one size ends and the next begins, and that is exactly why the maker's chart for the vest you order is the one that counts. A large in one maker's vest is 40–44 inches; in another maker's vest it is 44–46. Same letter, different vest — measure the chest and read that maker's chart.

Length matters too, and it is the second number on the chart. The single-letter spec sheet above runs from 25 inches long at S to 30 inches at 5X — about 64 cm to 76 cm. A vest should cover the waist and sit at the hip; a vest that ends at the waist rides up when the worker bends, and a vest that hangs past the hip catches on things. If the crew is tall, check the length as well as the chest.

4. Fit checks before you order

Four checks, done with the vest on and closed, over the clothing the worker will actually wear. First, the front closure meets without strain — no gap, no pulling, no zip or hook straining. Second, the reflective bands lie flat around the torso, without twisting or folding, so they show from every side. Third, the vest covers the waist and sits at the hip when the worker stands, and stays there when the worker bends and reaches up. Fourth, the armholes do not bind when the arms are raised — a vest that is right in the chest but tight in the armholes is the wrong size.

Two common mistakes cover most bad fits. The first is measuring over a T-shirt and wearing over a jacket: measure over the jacket, or order one size up for winter wear. The second is ordering the whole crew in one size: a crew's chests span a range, and a single size fits the middle and nobody else. The size run for a crew is worked out from the measurements — send the chest measurements, and the supplier confirms how many of each size to send. Pockets change the fit too: a vest with front pockets for tools and small items carries weight at the front, so check the fit with the pockets loaded, not empty.

The size guide in this article applies to all six the same way: the chest measurement is the same, and the fabric is the difference.

5. Ordering by size run

A crew order is a size run, not a single size. Send the chest measurements — or at least the count per size — with the inquiry, and the supplier confirms the run. Anything product-specific is confirmed at inquiry: the style, the quantities per size, the packing and the delivery. FAQ

What size vest do I need for a 110 cm chest? S/M. The combined-size table puts 91–112 cm in S/M, so 110 cm is inside S/M — near the top of it, so if you wear a thick jacket underneath, take L/XL.

Should I measure over a jacket? Yes, if the vest will be worn over a jacket. Measure over whatever will be under the vest on the job. A vest measured over a T-shirt will not close over a winter jacket.

What if my chest is between two sizes? Take the larger size. A vest a little loose still closes and still shows its bands; a vest a little tight gaps at the front.

Are vest sizes the same for every maker? No. Combined sizes are broadly similar, but single-letter sizes differ between makers — a large in one maker's vest is not a large in another's. Measure your chest and read the maker's chart for the vest you are ordering.

How long should a vest be? It should cover the waist and sit at the hip — about 25 inches (64 cm) at S to 30 inches (76 cm) at 5X on the published spec sheet used here. If it rides up when you bend, it is too short.

Does mesh or polyester change the size? No. The same chest measurement fits a mesh vest and a polyester vest. The fabric changes the breathability and the weight, not the size.
